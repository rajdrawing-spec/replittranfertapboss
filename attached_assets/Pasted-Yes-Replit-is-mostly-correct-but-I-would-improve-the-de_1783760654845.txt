Yes, **Replit is mostly correct**, but I would improve the design because TapasHub is intended to be an investment and business management platform.

### What Replit said

Example:

* Face Value = ₹100/share
* Shares = 769
* Investor Paid = ₹1,25,000

Legal Share Capital:

```
769 × ₹100 = ₹76,900
```

The remaining amount:

```
₹1,25,000 - ₹76,900 = ₹48,100
```

is **Share Premium**.

Accounting entry:

| Account              |    Amount |
| -------------------- | --------: |
| Share Capital        |   ₹76,900 |
| Share Premium        |   ₹48,100 |
| Total Money Received | ₹1,25,000 |

This follows standard corporate accounting.

---

# But for TapasHub, I recommend showing both values.

A shareholder wants to know:

* How much they invested.
* What their shares are currently worth.

Your certificate should include:

| Field                          |   Example |
| ------------------------------ | --------: |
| Number of Shares               |       769 |
| Face Value                     |      ₹100 |
| Issue Price per Share          |   ₹162.55 |
| Total Face Value               |   ₹76,900 |
| Total Amount Paid (Investment) | ₹1,25,000 |
| Share Premium                  |   ₹48,100 |
| Current Book Value             | ₹1,48,300 |
| Estimated Market Value         | ₹2,35,000 |
| Ownership                      |    12.45% |

This is much more useful than showing only face value.

## My recommendation for TapasHub

Since TapasHub is more than a legal record system—it's an AI-powered investment and business platform—you should **not hide the invested amount**. Keep the legal **face value** for compliance, but also display:

* Total Amount Paid (Investment)
* Share Premium
* Current Book Value
* Estimated Market Value (AI estimate, clearly labeled as an estimate)

This gives founders, shareholders, and investors a complete picture of both the legal share details and the financial performance of their investment.
